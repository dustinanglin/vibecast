Design proposal · 19 Nov 2026 · for review

Vibecast on iPhone Duo

How the app should lay out in each way the Duo can be held, based on Apple's “Designing for iPhone Duo” guidelines and the current 1.0.2 code. Nothing is built yet.

The one idea

Library + Stage

Today Vibecast is one screen (the Library: masthead and show list) with two things that cover it: the full-screen player and a show's episode list. On the Duo, whenever a second screen-half is available, those two get a permanent home beside the Library, called the Stage. The Stage shows Now Playing by default and a show's episodes when you open one. With one screen-half, everything works as it does today.

The gear, vibes and + buttons leave the masthead and become real toolbar items wherever the Duo puts its controls on the side.

Pose 1

Closed: buttons move to the side bar

The outer display is wider and shorter than any other iPhone, and iOS reserves a strip beside the camera for controls. Vibecast hides its navigation bar and draws its own buttons, so today that strip sits empty and the whole app looks pushed to the left.

  • Gear, Vibes and + become system toolbar items. Order from the top: + Add, Vibes, Settings. If space runs out, Settings goes into the overflow menu first.
  • The vibe colour band runs the full width, under the side bar. The list stays inside the safe area.
  • “Edit order” stays where it is. Apple keeps text-only buttons out of the side bar, and it belongs next to the list it edits.
  • The Now Playing sheet does not fit this height today (fixed 280 pt artwork, about 730 pt of content on a 678 pt screen). Artwork becomes flexible so everything fits without scrolling.
Poses 2 and 3

Open in landscape: Library on the left, Stage on the right

Opening the Duo like a book gives a landscape inner display. Apple's guidance is to keep the same hierarchy and reveal one more level of it. The left half is the screen you just had on the outer display, so nothing moves as you open it.

  • Stage default: Now Playing. Large artwork, scrubber, transport, and a short Up Next list drawn from the current vibe's order. The mini-player is hidden because it would duplicate the Stage.
  • Tap a show: its episode list opens on the Stage, not as a push. A Close button appears at the top of the side bar. The mini-player returns at the bottom of the Library, and tapping it brings the Stage back to Now Playing.
  • Nothing loaded yet: the Stage shows the splash motif with “Pick your vibe.”
  • Part-folded like a book: same layout. The system container keeps each pane clear of the fold, so no custom fold handling is needed here.
  • Add Podcast, Manage Vibes and Settings open as sheets placed over the Stage half, so the Library stays visible and you can watch shows arrive as you add them.
Pose 4

Open in portrait: the same single screen, larger

As you suggested, this is the phone layout with more room. Apple keeps standard horizontal bars in this pose, so the masthead keeps its three buttons exactly as on other iPhones.

  • More rows visible. Margins grow from 22 to 28 pt; type sizes stay the same.
  • The Now Playing sheet uses the taller layout with artwork up to about 360 pt.
Pose 5

Part-folded in portrait: list above, player deck below

Set on a desk like a small laptop, the upper half faces you and the lower half lies flat under your fingers. The Library stays on top, where it already was in portrait, and the Stage appears on the lower half as a player deck.

  • Folding from open portrait only adds the Stage. The Library does not move, which follows Apple's advice to avoid large rearrangements as the device folds.
  • The masthead tightens in a half-height pane (smaller wordmark, play pill moves beside it) so four rows stay visible.
  • Now Playing switches to a side-by-side layout: artwork left, title and controls right. Opening a show replaces the deck with its episodes, as in landscape.

Your call: the alternative is Now Playing on the upper half (artwork as an ambient display) with controls and the list below. It looks better on a desk but rearranges the whole screen each time you fold. I recommend the version drawn here.

How it gets built

Phase 0 · Check the assumptions (half a day)

  • In DeviceHub: inner display point size, and which poses report a side bar.
  • Does a portrait-only app get the landscape inner display, or must landscape be enabled?
  • Do toolbar items appear in the side bar while the navigation bar title is hidden?
  • Move CI from Xcode 26 to Xcode 27. Builds from Xcode 26 do not use the full Duo screen.

Phase 1 · Closed pose (ships on its own)

  • Toolbar items for the three buttons, shown only where a side bar exists.
  • Full-width colour band.
  • Flexible Now Playing layout, shared by sheet and Stage.
  • Icon and title on every sheet toolbar button.

Phase 2 · Library + Stage in landscape

  • Two-pane container, horizontal only.
  • Stage state: Now Playing, show detail, or empty.
  • Carry state across open and close: a pushed show or open player sheet becomes the Stage, and back again.

Phase 3 · Laptop pose and polish

  • Vertical split when the fold is active in portrait.
  • Tight masthead, side-by-side Now Playing.
  • Sheets over the Stage half; splash wordmark kept off the fold.

The app still supports iOS 26.4, so every Duo-specific API sits behind an iOS 27 availability check and other iPhones keep today's layout unchanged. The builder's version of this proposal is in the repo at docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-iphone-duo-design.md.

Decisions I made that you should check

  1. Laptop pose: Library on top, player deck below (alternative described above).
  2. Stage default is Now Playing, with a read-only Up Next list. Up Next is new; it can be dropped without affecting the rest.
  3. Masthead buttons stay on ordinary iPhones and in open portrait. They move to the side bar only where the system has one. The alternative is system toolbars everywhere on iOS 27, which is simpler but changes the look on every iPhone.
  4. Ordinary iPhones stay portrait-only. Landscape is for the Duo's inner display. This depends on the Phase 0 orientation check.
  5. Side bar order: Add, Vibes, Settings, with Settings first to overflow.
